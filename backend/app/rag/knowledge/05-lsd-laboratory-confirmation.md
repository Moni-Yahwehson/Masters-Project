## Confirmation is a laboratory decision

Clinical and image-based assessment can raise a **suspicion** of LSD. Only
laboratory testing confirms it. Because LSD is a WOAH-listed disease with trade
consequences, confirmation must be performed by the competent veterinary
authority or an authorised laboratory — never by the herd owner and never on the
basis of photographs alone.

## Samples to collect

- **Skin biopsy or nodule material.** The sample of choice. Take a full-thickness
  biopsy of a nodule, ideally before the necrotic plug separates. Split the
  sample: one portion fresh or frozen for virus detection, one in 10 per cent
  neutral buffered formalin for histopathology.
- **Scabs and crusts.** Easy to collect non-invasively and contain very high
  virus loads. Excellent for PCR.
- **EDTA whole blood.** Useful only during the viraemic window, roughly the
  febrile phase and first days of nodule development.
- **Clotted blood / serum.** For antibody detection; take paired acute and
  convalescent samples about 3–4 weeks apart.
- **Nasal, ocular and oral swabs.** Useful in animals with mucosal lesions.
- **Semen**, where transmission through breeding is suspected.

Samples should be shipped chilled, with a clear history: number of animals
affected, morbidity, mortality, vaccination status, movement history, and the
date lesions were first seen.

## Detection methods

- **Real-time PCR.** The primary confirmatory test — rapid, sensitive and
  specific for capripoxvirus DNA. Differentiating assays can distinguish LSDV
  from sheeppox and goatpox virus, and specific assays can distinguish wild-type
  field strains from live attenuated vaccine strains, which matters when a
  vaccinated animal develops lesions.
- **Conventional PCR and sequencing.** Used for strain characterisation and
  outbreak tracing.
- **Virus isolation** in cell culture (for example lamb testis or bovine dermis
  cells), followed by identification. Slower, and used mainly for
  characterisation and for producing isolates.
- **Electron microscopy** of nodule material, which reveals characteristic
  poxvirus particles but does not distinguish the capripoxviruses.
- **Histopathology.** Shows vasculitis, dermal necrosis and eosinophilic
  intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies in keratinocytes and macrophages —
  supportive rather than definitive.

## Serology and its limits

Virus neutralisation is the reference serological test; ELISAs are available for
herd-level screening. Serology has two important limitations: capripoxvirus
antibodies cross-react, so LSD cannot be separated from sheep and goat pox
serologically, and vaccinated animals test positive. Antibody responses can also
be weak or short-lived in mildly affected animals, so a negative serological
result does not exclude infection.
