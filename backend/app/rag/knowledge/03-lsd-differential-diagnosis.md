## Why differentials matter for image-based assessment

Many cattle skin conditions produce nodules or lumps. Photographs show
morphology and distribution but not fever, lymph node size, palpation depth or
laboratory results. Every one of the conditions below has been mistaken for LSD
in the field, so an image-only assessment must be framed as a screening
suspicion, never a confirmed diagnosis.

## Pseudo-lumpy skin disease (bovine herpesvirus 2)

The closest mimic. BHV-2 causes superficial skin lesions that begin as raised
plaques and evolve into a characteristic ring-shaped scab. Distinguishing
features: the lesions are **superficial** rather than full-thickness, the course
is milder and shorter (usually resolving in one to three weeks), and systemic
signs are mild or absent. Laboratory differentiation is required.

## Dermatophilosis (*Dermatophilus congolensis*, "rain scald")

Produces matted tufts of hair over crusted, exudative lesions along the topline
and rump, typically after prolonged wetting. The crusts are superficial and lift
off with a plug of hair and a moist underside. There are no deep intradermal
nodules and no generalised lymph node enlargement.

## Dermatophytosis (ringworm)

Circular, well-demarcated, grey-white scaly patches of hair loss, most often on
the head and neck of young stock. The lesions are flat or slightly raised and
scaly rather than firm, deep and dome-shaped.

## Insect and tick bite reactions, and urticaria

Multiple small raised swellings that can superficially resemble early LSD
nodules. Bite reactions are usually soft, transient, concentrated where vectors
feed, and resolve within days. Allergic urticaria produces soft, oedematous,
rapidly appearing weals that often respond quickly to antihistamines or
corticosteroids and are not accompanied by fever or lymphadenopathy.

## Besnoitiosis (*Besnoitia besnoiti*)

Causes thickened, wrinkled, hairless skin and the pathognomonic sand-like
scleral and conjunctival cysts visible on the eye. The skin change is diffuse
thickening rather than discrete round nodules.

## Demodicosis (*Demodex* mange)

Small, firm nodules, generally 0.5–1 cm, most often over the shoulders, neck and
brisket. Nodules contain caseous material and mites, and are typically smaller
and more uniform than LSD nodules. Skin scraping is diagnostic.

## Onchocerciasis and other parasitic nodules

Firm subcutaneous nodules at characteristic sites, without fever or the acute
systemic illness that accompanies LSD.

## Bovine farcy, actinobacillosis and cutaneous tuberculosis

Produce chronic nodules that follow lymphatic chains, often ulcerate and
discharge purulent material, and progress over months rather than days.

## Photosensitisation and sunburn

Affect non-pigmented, poorly haired skin. Lesions are erythematous, oedematous
and later crusted or sloughing, confined to light-coloured areas — a
distribution pattern unlike the generalised, colour-independent spread of LSD.

## Rinderpest-era and other systemic differentials

Bovine papular stomatitis, malignant catarrhal fever and bovine viral diarrhoea
can produce mucosal ulceration that overlaps with the mucosal component of LSD,
but they do not produce the characteristic generalised deep skin nodules.
